# Experiments that surprised classical physics
## Stern-Gerlach experiment
Silver atoms travel through inhomogeneous magnetic field.
- Deflect up or down, depending on their spin (no other directions!)
- Quantum states are found

## Double-slit Experiment
Photons go through the slits; The place where each photon lands on is probabilisitic: Some places are very likely, while some are not.

One slit open + One slit open != Two slit open !
-> The photon interfere with itself, making a specific probability distribution, rather then just simply applying additivity rule.

## Modified Probablilty Theory
- Probablilties: amplitudes $`\in \mathbb{C}`$; the norm of amplitude must equal 1
- Sequence in independent setps -> mult amplitudes
- Happen in several alternative ways -> add amplitudes

$`p = |\alpha|^2 = p_1 + p_2 + 2 \sqrt{p_1p_2}\cos(\phi_2 - \phi_1)`$
where $`\alpha_i = |\alpha_i|e^{\phi_i}`$
the term with cosine is called 'interference term'
